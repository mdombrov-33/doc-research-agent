The Transmission Control Protocol, or TCP, establishes a reliable connection between two
hosts before any application data is exchanged. This setup procedure is known as the
three-way handshake, because it involves three messages passed between the client and the
server.

The handshake begins when the client sends a segment with the SYN flag set, short for
synchronize. This first message proposes an initial sequence number that the client will
use to order the bytes it sends. The server replies with a segment that has both the SYN
and ACK flags set: the ACK acknowledges the client's sequence number, and the SYN carries
the server's own initial sequence number. Finally, the client responds with an ACK segment
that acknowledges the server's sequence number. After this third message, the connection is
established and both sides may send data.

Sequence and acknowledgment numbers let TCP detect lost or out-of-order segments and
retransmit them, which is what makes the protocol reliable. When the conversation is over,
the connection is closed with a separate exchange of FIN and ACK segments. The deliberate
handshake is also why establishing a TCP connection adds a full round trip of latency
before any useful data can flow.
