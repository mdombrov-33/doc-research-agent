Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system and has dozens of known moons, but four of
them stand out for their size. These are Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto, collectively
called the Galilean moons because the astronomer Galileo Galilei discovered them in 1610
using one of the first telescopes. Their discovery provided early evidence that not
everything in the heavens orbits the Earth.

Each of the four is a distinct world. Io is the most volcanically active body in the solar
system, its surface constantly reshaped by eruptions driven by tidal heating. Europa is
covered by a smooth shell of ice that is thought to hide a salty ocean of liquid water
beneath, making it a leading target in the search for life. Ganymede is the largest moon in
the entire solar system, even bigger than the planet Mercury, and it is the only moon known
to generate its own magnetic field. Callisto, the outermost of the four, is one of the most
heavily cratered objects known, its ancient surface a record of billions of years of impacts.

The Galilean moons orbit Jupiter in a few days to a couple of weeks and can be seen from
Earth with ordinary binoculars, appearing as small points of light strung out in a line
beside the bright planet.
